**Liquidity mining** is the practice of distributing additional token rewards to participants who provide liquidity or perform another desired onchain action. It is commonly used to bootstrap markets, attract initial depth, or direct liquidity toward a protocol’s preferred pools.

The mechanism can be useful. It also changes the economics of the position and should be analyzed separately from organic trading fees.

## What incentives are trying to solve

A new market can struggle with a cold-start problem: traders want good execution, but LPs want evidence of trading activity before committing inventory. Token rewards can subsidize the early phase by compensating LPs while the market builds awareness and usage.

This can improve access to liquidity. It can also attract capital that is primarily there to collect rewards rather than serve durable demand.

## Separate fee income from emissions

A pool’s dashboard might display a combined APR. That number can include swap fees, protocol rewards, partner rewards, and assumptions about token prices. Each component has a different source and persistence profile.

| Return component | Primary driver | Key question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trading fees | User and arbitrage volume | Is the volume likely to persist? |
| Protocol rewards | Token emissions | What is the schedule and dilution path? |
| Partner rewards | External program | Who funds it and for how long? |
| Price movement | Asset relationship | What inventory outcome does the pool create? |

## The durability test

A useful thought experiment is to remove the reward program. Would traders still need this pool? Would LPs still receive meaningful fees? Would local depth remain competitive? If the answer is no, the incentive may be buying rented liquidity rather than helping establish a self-sustaining market.

That is not necessarily a reason to dismiss the pool. It is a reason to avoid treating the advertised rate as a permanent property.

## Governance matters

Rewards can be increased, reduced, redirected, or ended. Token governance, treasury decisions, voting concentration, and unlock schedules all influence the expected value of an incentive. An LP position can look very different when an emission schedule changes, even if the pool’s smart contract has not changed at all.

## The takeaway

Liquidity mining is a **subsidy layer**, not a substitute for market fit. The strongest programs use incentives to accelerate a pool that already has a credible reason to exist. The weakest programs use emissions to obscure the absence of organic demand.

Read the rewards as a policy decision with a timetable, not as a yield instrument with a fixed coupon.
