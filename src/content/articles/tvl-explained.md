**Total value locked**, commonly shortened to TVL, is a dollar-denominated estimate of assets held inside a protocol or pool. It is a useful visibility metric because it offers a quick sense of scale. It is not, by itself, a measure of quality, safety, or likely returns.

The phrase “locked” can also be misleading. In many DeFi systems, assets can be withdrawn subject to the rules of the contract. TVL describes where assets sit at a point in time; it does not necessarily describe how permanent those assets are.

## What TVL captures

At its simplest, TVL is calculated by multiplying the token balances in a contract by reference prices, then adding the values together. At a pool level, that may reflect the inventory available to support trades. At a protocol level, it can include liquidity pools, collateral vaults, staking contracts, or other categories.

Higher TVL can sometimes mean a larger trade can be executed with less price impact. But that relationship depends on where liquidity is located and how the curve is configured.

## Why TVL can mislead

A TVL figure can rise because the underlying assets rose in price, even if no new capital arrived. It can also be boosted by incentive programs that attract short-term liquidity, by double-counting assets that are used as collateral elsewhere, or by including assets with unstable price references.

| TVL question | Better companion metric |
| --- | --- |
| Is the pool usable for trading? | Depth near the current price and realized slippage |
| Is the capital durable? | Deposit and withdrawal history, incentive dependence |
| Is the protocol active? | Volume, active users, and fee generation |
| Is the asset composition stable? | Asset concentration and collateral quality |

## Read TVL in context

A pool with less TVL but liquidity concentrated near the active price could offer better execution for a specific trade than a larger pool with inactive or widely distributed liquidity. Conversely, a large TVL number may tell you that the pool has accumulated significant reserves but reveal little about the risk of those reserves.

TVL is best used as a starting point for further questions: What assets create the number? How has it changed? How much trading occurs against it? Is it supported by organic use or temporary rewards?

## The takeaway

TVL is a map scale, not the terrain. It can help you narrow a research universe, but a serious view of a liquidity pool requires structure, volume, fee flow, asset risk, and governance context alongside the headline value.
